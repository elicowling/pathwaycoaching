The problem

The topic of the issue is not the issue.

I have watched this play out over and over. Everyone in the room knows something is wrong. Nobody says it. So the team moves forward mitigating the symptoms of the issue rather than solving it, and six months later everyone is asking why we have not gotten anywhere.

It happens at home too. Two people who love each other, both guessing, assuming, and avoiding the real issue.

Left alone, this does not get better. It either gets worse, or we slowly accept that it is “just the way it is”.

“Leaders don’t need someone to solve their issue. They need the courage and clarity to act on what they already know.”

The cornerstone

Open + Honest = Real Communication

Know Honesty™ sits underneath everything I do, with leaders, with teams and with couples. It is simple. Simple is not the same as easy.

Honesty

Being truly and freely yourself™, speaking into what you want and how you feel.

Openness

Listening without reservation™, putting your needs and wants on pause for someone else.

There are a lot of communication tools. Most of them are really about negotiation. They help you survive a difficult conversation when one shows up. This is different. Real conversations all the time, instead of difficult conversations sometimes.

The plan

Three steps. That is the whole thing.

  1. Start with a conversation

    Thirty minutes, free, nothing to prepare. You tell me what is actually going on, and I ask questions.

  2. Find your Foundation

    Your foundational core value. The one thing you measure decisions against. Once you can name it, a lot of hard calls stop being hard.

  3. Know / Want / Do™

    What do you truly Know? What do you really Want? What are you willing and able to Do? Then you go do it, and we adjust as you go.

What this looks like

“I’ll wait.”

A client was taking on too much. He had just committed to leading a group every Wednesday night, the one night he had with his family. We worked through what needed to happen, and he said, “Yeah, I need to let them know I won’t be attending.”

I said, “Great. I’ll wait.”

He was confused at first, then he understood. He pulled out his phone and texted them on the spot.

That is the job. Not more information. Action, while it is still hot.
